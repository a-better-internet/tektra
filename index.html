    How the machine works

    The architect
    You do not play notes. You design a web of modules and the rules that connect them, then switch it on. The music is whatever the web does.
    A closed loop
    There are no open ends. The loop’s output returns to the tape, to the last multiplier and to the generators’ phase, and reels record the output back into the system.
    Multiplication, not addition
    Four ring multipliers run in series. A product cancels the frequencies that went in and leaves their sums and differences, so one small change shatters into many sidebands.
    Multiplied feedback
    Inside the loop sit a tape delay, a bank of third-octave filters, a single-sideband frequency shifter and a smearing reverberator. Every pass is re-carved, re-pitched and smeared.
    Eyes
    Envelope followers read levels, spectral balance, change and regulation pressure. They get used to what they see, so they report deviation, and the matrix turns that into control voltage.
    Homeostasis
    Every band has an inverted VCA that holds it down when it grows too loud and lets go slowly. The loop as a whole inhales toward the setpoint and exhales past it, so it reaches the brink, saves itself and grows back changed.
    No predictable tools
    There are no LFOs, no noise generators and no step sequencers. Rhythm and events come from oscillators pulling toward shared ratios, gates flipping and loops fighting for stability.
    Tapes and logic
    Reels hold concrete material: the system’s own past, or a sound you load. Logic gates compare what the eyes see and decide when reels record and which come up in the mix.
    Cosmic time
    Era stretches every slow process, from weather (seconds) to aeons (tens of minutes). Systems like this need hours to show what they are. The chronicle keeps the whole run.
    The steersman
    The helm nudges: inflow, bias, drift, setpoint, coupling, era. Every move glides, so you push the system out of one equilibrium and watch it organise itself into another.

    Space engage or halt · P perturb · Knobs: drag, arrow keys, or scroll while focused · double-click resets

    Nothing in this instrument draws a random number while it runs. The same architecture, left alone, takes the same path.
